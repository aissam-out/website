Speaker embeddings turn a voice into a fixed vector so you can compare people across recordings. Cosine similarity then becomes a cheap way to ask whether two utterances likely came from the same speaker.

The trap is that the embedding model will happily encode garbage. A clipped fragment, a mostly silent crop, a diarization error that mixes two talkers, or telephone noise can still yield a perfectly shaped 256-d vector. Syntactically valid is not the same as biometrically meaningful.

Production systems therefore need a quality gate before indexing: usable duration, silence, clipping, and often an audio-quality model. Thresholds for “same speaker” are a product decision too. High precision usually matters more than recall when a false match has real consequences.

If you treat every embedding as evidence, your vector database becomes a museum of confident mistakes. Filter first, then search.
